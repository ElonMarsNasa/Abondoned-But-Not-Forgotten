A journey to the icy north

Phoenix landed in Mars’s northern plains on May 25, 2008. Its mission: investigate the icy soil and the history of water.

What lies beneath?

Phoenix’s arm scraped away the surface. Could the bright material underneath be ice?

A layer of soil hides what lies below.

The disappearing clue

Bright pieces in a trench were visible on June 15, 2008. Four days later, some had disappeared. Scientists interpreted this as exposed water ice turning directly into vapor.

Before: bright pieces lie inside the freshly dug trench.

A tiny oven, a big discovery

Phoenix delivered samples to small ovens. Heating released gases that instruments could analyze. Laboratory results confirmed water ice.

Collect a sample for the laboratory.

Could this place support life?

Water is part of the puzzle. Soil chemistry and environmental conditions matter too.

Water ice
Phoenix confirmed water ice just below the surface. Ice records part of the region’s water history.
A chemical puzzle
The soil contained salts, including perchlorate. Scientists investigated what those chemicals meant for the environment.
What about life?
Phoenix studied whether the site could have been habitable. It did not discover life.

Winter approaches

Shorter days and colder conditions reduced the power available to Phoenix. Its last signal arrived on November 2, 2008.

During the working season, sunlight powered the lander’s investigations.
