SURVEYOR 3

A small scoop. Big clues for a Moon landing.

Meet a robotic explorer that helped prepare the way for Apollo.

A scout for future explorers

Surveyor 3 launched on April 17, 1967. Its destination was the Moon’s Ocean of Storms.

The mission would test landing technology and investigate lunar soil.

A landing with two bounces

A delayed engine cutoff caused two bounces before Surveyor 3 settled on April 20, 1967.

The spacecraft approaches the lunar surface.

Earth gets a closer look

The camera returned more than 6,000 pictures of the surrounding terrain.

Footpads and nearby ground helped engineers assess the landing.

Would the ground support a lander?

The surface sampler dug, pressed, and moved soil to test its mechanical properties.

Commands from Earth guided the sampler.

A long lunar night

Sunlight powered the lander. Lunar night interrupted its work, and contact ended on May 4, 1967.

Daylight supplies energy through the solar panel.

A robot gets human visitors

In November 1969, Apollo 12’s Conrad and Bean visited the inactive Surveyor 3.

Apollo 12 landed within walking distance.

The robot that helped people follow

Its soil tests supported Apollo planning. Returned parts let scientists study exposure on the Moon.
