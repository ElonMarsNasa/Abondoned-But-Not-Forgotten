INGENUITY

Built for five test flights. It flew 72 times.

A little helicopter took on a big challenge: flying on another planet.

A passenger beneath a rover

Ingenuity traveled beneath Perseverance and arrived on Mars on February 18, 2021. Its job was to prove that powered, controlled flight could work there.

THE ENGINEERING CHALLENGE
A light body and large, fast-spinning rotors made flight possible.

The moment it lifted off

April 19, 2021. Ingenuity rose 3 meters, hovered for 30 seconds, and landed: the first powered, controlled flight on another planet.

Ready on the ground. Follow the takeoff, hover, and landing.

Flying without a pilot nearby

Engineers planned the flights on Earth. Ingenuity used its own software, sensors, and camera images to guide itself during flight.

→→→
Plan: the team chooses a flight path and checks the conditions.

Five flights became 72

A short technology test grew into an extended adventure. Ingenuity later provided aerial views to help the Perseverance team scout terrain.

Flight 1: a short rise and hover proved that controlled flight on Mars was possible.

One final landing

Ingenuity flew for the last time on January 18, 2024. Damage to its rotor blades left it unable to fly again.

A flight can end and still teach us.

Engineers investigated what happened during the landing. Understanding the limits of a small aircraft helps guide the design of future explorers.
