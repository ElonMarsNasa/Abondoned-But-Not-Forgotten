Level 1 · A name from a kid

A girl looked at the stars

Sofi Collis, age 9, from ArizonaNearly 10,000 essays in the contestHer names: Spirit and Opportunity

In 2003, NASA and the LEGO company held a contest to name two Mars rovers. In her essay, Sofi wrote that the sparkly night sky made her feel better and made her dream she could fly there. Her winning words became the names of two robots.

Who is Spirit?
A robot geologist on Mars, about the size of a golf cart, with six wheels, solar panels and cameras for eyes. She launched on June 10, 2003 and landed in January 2004, bouncing in airbags into Gusev Crater. Her twin, Opportunity, explored the other side of Mars.
SCIENCE
Spirit and Opportunity carried the same science tools. Their shared question: what could rocks and soil tell us about ancient water on Mars?

Level 2 · A challenging journey

A little rover, a big adventure

Tap all three stops to follow Spirit across the hills and discover how her team helped her keep exploring.

Columbia HillsHusband Hill · 2005

A hill on the horizon became a destination. Spirit crossed rocky ground and climbed up for a new view.

SCIENCE
Spirit became the first robot to reach the summit of a hill on another planet. Her cameras helped scientists study the landscape.
Right-front wheel failed · 2006Silica discovery · 2007

Her team found a way to drive backward on five working wheels. The dragging wheel scraped open bright soil.

SCIENCE
Tests showed that the pale material was rich in silica. A difficult mechanical problem had helped uncover an important clue.
Troy · 2009

Near Home Plate, Spirit sank into loose ground. Engineers tested escape ideas, but she could not get free.

SCIENCE
A rover needs traction: its wheels must grip the ground. Loose soil gave way beneath Spirit, and winter was approaching.

Science stop · An unexpected discovery

What was hiding under the dust?

A scraped patch of soil gave scientists a new clue about water and heat on ancient Mars.

Mars today: cold, dry and dusty.

Follow the evidence

Silica-rich soil

In 2007, Spirit found soil containing about 90% silica. On Earth, hot water or steam can concentrate this mineral.

A warmer, wetter past

Scientists proposed ancient hot springs or steam vents. Places like these on Earth can support tiny living things.

Home Plate rocks

Layered rocks nearby preserve evidence of explosive activity. Spirit helped reveal how water and volcanic heat may have interacted.

WHAT DOES IT MEAN?
Water clues help scientists explore whether Mars once had suitable environments for life. Spirit did not discover living things.
Level 3 · A quiet winter

One last Martian winter

March 22, 2010: last contactMay 25, 2011: mission complete

Stuck in place, Spirit faced a winter with less sunlight to power her panels. Her radio fell silent. The team kept listening and trying to reach her.

SCIENCE
A solar rover needs energy to run its equipment and keep vital parts warm. NASA ended recovery attempts in May 2011.
