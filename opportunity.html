Level 1 · A name from a kid

A girl looked at the stars

Sofi Collis, age 9, from ArizonaNearly 10,000 essays in the contestHer names: Spirit and Opportunity

In 2003, NASA and the LEGO company held a contest to name two Mars rovers. In her essay, Sofi wrote that the sparkly night sky made her feel better and made her dream she could fly there. Her winning words became the names of two robots.

Who is Opportunity?
A robot geologist on Mars, about the size of a golf cart, with six wheels, solar panels and cameras for eyes. She launched on July 7, 2003 (Pacific time) and landed on January 25, 2004 (UTC; January 24 in California), bouncing in airbags into Eagle Crater at Meridiani Planum. Her twin, Spirit, explored the other side of Mars.
SCIENCE
Orbiters had spotted a mineral called hematite on Meridiani Planum. On Earth it often forms near water, so NASA sent her there to ask: was Mars once wet?

Level 2 · The long road

Hard roads are okay

Opportunity faced many hard times. Tap each stop to see how she kept going.

NASA's plan: about 3 monthsWhat happened: over 14 years exploring

Her planned mission was 90 sols. She was still exploring more than 14 Earth years after landing. Recovery efforts continued until February 2019.

SCIENCE
NASA counts time on Mars in sols. One sol is one Mars day, about 24 hours 40 minutes. NASA planned 90 sols (about 3 months) and she worked for 5,111 sols. Wind gusts and dust devils sometimes blew dust off her solar panels, which helped a lot.
Start: Eagle CraterFinish: Perseverance Valley, Endeavour’s rim
About 113 laps of a 400 m school trackPast a marathon distance in March 2015
SCIENCE
At Endeavour she found clay minerals, evidence of less acidic ancient water. On the way she also found Heat Shield Rock, an iron-nickel meteorite, in 2005.
Parked on a slope that faces the sunFrost and freezing nights

Winter nights on Mars are far colder than a freezer, and the sunlight gets weak. Her team on Earth kept solving problems for her.

SCIENCE
Engineers used sun-facing slopes to improve winter power. A right-front wheel sometimes drew extra electrical current, so driving backward helped reduce wear. The team also adapted her operations when her flash memory failed.

A careful escape: In April 2005 she became stuck at Purgatory Dune. After sandbox tests on Earth and weeks of careful driving, her team freed her. She also survived a major dust storm in 2007 by reducing activity.

Science stop · Was Mars wet?

Imagine Mars with water

Opportunity found clues that liquid water once covered places on Mars. Travel back in time and see how scientists imagine it.

Mars today: cold, dry and dusty.

Real clues from Opportunity's cameras

Blueberries up close
This image covers an area 3 cm across—not the width of each tiny ball. These hematite-rich mineral concretions formed through groundwater activity.
The Berry Bowl rock
The spheres occur within the rock as well as loose on the ground. Studying their mineral composition helped scientists test how water changed these rocks.
Blueberries everywhere
Similar spheres lie scattered across the plains. Comparing these deposits helped scientists investigate the region’s watery past.
MORE CLUES
Ripple-shaped layers and sulfate minerals add to the evidence for ancient water. Near Endeavour Crater, the bright Homestake vein contained gypsum: a mineral deposited as water moved through cracks. Clay minerals pointed to a less acidic watery environment.
A PLACE FOR LIFE?
These clues suggest that some ancient environments could have supported microbes. Opportunity found evidence of past water and potentially habitable conditions; she did not discover life.
Level 3 · The great dust storm

The sky went dark

June 10, 2018: last contactFebruary 13, 2019: mission complete

In 2018, a planet-wide dust storm blocked the sunlight Opportunity needed for power. Her last signal arrived on June 10. NASA sent more than 1,000 commands during recovery efforts before ending the mission on February 13, 2019.

SCIENCE
You may have seen the line "My battery is low and it's getting dark". It was a poetic paraphrase by a journalist, not a real transmission.
What Opportunity made possible
Opportunity: solar panelsCuriosity (2012): nuclear battery, tall mastPerseverance (2021): nuclear battery, helicopter friend

She returned more than 217,000 images, including 15 full-color panoramas. Her water discoveries, navigation methods, and the experience of her team helped later Mars missions. Her exploration ended, but her data and discoveries live on.

A record-setting journey: Opportunity’s 45.16 km drive set a record beyond Earth. NASA reports that Perseverance surpassed that total on October 11, 2026.
