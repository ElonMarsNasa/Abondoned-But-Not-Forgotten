Follow the spacecraft that orbited—and touched down on—an asteroid first.

A journey to a small world

NEAR launched on February 17, 1996. Its destination was Eros, with a flyby of Mathilde along the way.

NEAR means Near Earth Asteroid Rendezvous.

A quick look at Mathilde

On June 27, 1997, NEAR flew past Mathilde. The encounter revealed a dark asteroid with enormous craters.

A flyby gives a brief view as a spacecraft passes a world.

A setback became a new route

An engine burn was aborted in December 1998. The team revised the flight plan: NEAR flew past Eros, then prepared for another approach.

The first approach did not lead to the planned orbit.

An asteroid gets its first orbiter

On February 14, 2000, NEAR entered orbit around Eros—the first spacecraft to orbit an asteroid.

A careful approach allowed repeated observations of the same world.

More than a photograph

Different instruments answered different questions. Together, their measurements helped reveal Eros’s shape and composition.

Camera: images reveal craters, ridges, and surface details.

An orbiter tries a landing

NEAR was not designed as a lander. After a year studying Eros, engineers guided a careful descent on February 12, 2001.

A final approach to the surface, with no landing legs.

Touchdown was not the end

NEAR survived and continued sending useful data from the surface. Last contact: February 28, 2001.
