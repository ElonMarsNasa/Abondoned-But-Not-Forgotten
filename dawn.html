Beyond Mars

Dawn launched on September 27, 2007. Its destinations were two very different worlds in the main asteroid belt.

The asteroid belt lies between Mars and Jupiter.

A gentle push that adds up

Dawn used electricity to accelerate xenon ions. The gentle thrust could reshape its journey when applied for long periods.

Solar arrays provide electricity for ion propulsion.

First stop: Vesta

Dawn entered orbit around Vesta in July 2011. Its images revealed a rugged world marked by enormous impacts.

A huge basin records a powerful impact in Vesta’s past.

A connection to Earth

Some meteorites found on Earth came from Vesta. Dawn’s mineral measurements helped establish that connection.

Onward to Ceres

Dawn left Vesta and reached Ceres in March 2015. It became the first spacecraft to orbit two destinations beyond Earth.

Leaving one orbit makes a second destination possible.

The mystery of the bright spots

Bright patches stood out on Ceres. Closer observations and mineral measurements helped scientists investigate them.

From far away, small bright spots catch the eye.

Two worlds, one remarkable journey

Vesta

A rocky world with large impact scars and a connection to meteorites on Earth.

Ceres

A dwarf planet with salt deposits that reveal a history involving briny water.

Dawn’s mission ended in 2018 after it exhausted the hydrazine used to control its orientation. It could no longer point its antenna toward Earth.
